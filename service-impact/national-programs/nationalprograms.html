A Tradition of Service

Programs Built to Meet the Moment

Since 1922, Sigma Gamma Rho Sorority, Inc. has served communities nationally and abroad. The Sorority works alongside national organizations and community partners to strengthen educational access, health outcomes, youth development, safety, and quality of life.

Each national program focuses that commitment on an area where intentional service can create measurable, lasting progress.

National Program 01

Operation BigBookBag

Removing educational barriers by placing the tools students need directly into their hands.

Operation BigBookBag addresses the needs of school-aged children who are educationally at risk, including youth served by shelters, schools, hospitals, and extended-care facilities. Chapters collect and distribute educational materials, equipment, studying aids, and school supplies.

Established in 1996 under Dr. LaRona J. Morris, 18th International Grand Basileus, the program has expanded to include STEM exposure, tutoring, mentoring, and year-round educational support.

National Program 02

Women's Wellness Initiative

Supporting informed, empowered decisions about physical, mental, and emotional health.

The Women's Wellness Initiative focuses attention on health concerns that impact women, particularly women of color. Program areas include breast cancer awareness, intimate and domestic violence, heart health, diabetes, mental health, and other issues affecting women's well-being.

The initiative encourages healthy lifestyles, informed health decisions, risk reduction, and stronger representation of African American women in health research.

National Program 03

Swim 1922

Increasing water safety, swimming access, and confidence in communities historically excluded from aquatic participation.

Swim 1922 addresses racial disparities in swimming and drowning by providing opportunities for youth and adults to learn water safety and swimming skills. Through the partnership with USA Swimming, members and swimming professionals bring instruction and exposure directly into local communities.

National Program 04

Project CRADLE Care

Advancing healthier maternal and infant outcomes through education, advocacy, outreach, and awareness.

Project CRADLE Care is designed to raise awareness of inequitable maternal and infant health outcomes affecting Black women. The program combines community outreach, education, advocacy, and implicit-bias awareness to address disparities that can affect families across generations.

Through collaboration with March of Dimes, the program connects communities with information, support, and resources intended to strengthen maternal and infant health.

Maternal Health Infant Health Advocacy Health Equity
Program in Action

National Program 05

Annual Youth Symposium

A nationwide day of relevant, hands-on programming designed to empower young people and strengthen their futures.

Held by alumnae chapters on the second Saturday of March during Sigma Week, the Annual Youth Symposium addresses issues affecting young people through interactive education, leadership development, and a relevant annual theme. Undergraduate chapters and affiliates support the nationwide effort.

Originated under Dr. LaRona J. Morris during her 1996–2000 administration, the program continues with a standardized agenda designed to support educational and behavioral outcomes for youth.

A Broader Service Legacy

Service Through the Decades

Today's national programs continue a service tradition that has taken many forms. Sigma Gamma Rho's broader history includes initiatives such as Project Reassurance, Project Africa, and Project Mwanamugimu, alongside decades of local chapter programming and collaboration with national organizations.

The programs evolve. The obligation remains: Greater Service, Greater Progress.
