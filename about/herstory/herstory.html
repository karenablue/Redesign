Our History. Our Legacy. Our Future.

Herstory

Founded by seven educators in 1922, Sigma Gamma Rho Sorority, Incorporated has spent more than a century turning scholarship, sisterhood, leadership, and service into sustained progress.

1922

The Beginning

A Vision Born in 1922

On November 12, 1922, seven young educators in Indianapolis established Sigma Gamma Rho Sorority at Butler University.

Their decision was deliberate. They built an organization grounded in scholarship, mutual responsibility, leadership, and public service at a time when Black women faced sharply limited access to institutional power and opportunity.

What began with seven educators became a national institution and, eventually, an international sisterhood.

Seven educators. One purpose. A legacy still in motion.

Distinct by History

Firsts & Distinctions

Milestones and traditions that distinguish Sigma Gamma Rho within the history of Black Greek-letter organizations.

01

Founded at a Predominantly White Institution

Sigma Gamma Rho is the only NPHC sorority founded at a predominantly white institution — Butler University in Indianapolis.

02

Professional-Educator Roots

The Sorority began as a professional organization created by Black women educators whose work centered education, advancement, and service.

03

Early NPHC Organizing Participation

Sigma Gamma Rho participated in the 1928 Indianapolis discussions that preceded the permanent National Pan-Hellenic Council and became a full NPHC member in 1937.

04

Affiliate Tradition

Sigma Gamma Rho developed structured affiliate organizations that extended service and leadership beyond initiated members, including the Philo Affiliate for women in the community and the Rhoer Club for young women.

05

Philo Affiliate

The Philo Affiliate traces its beginnings to Galveston, Texas, in 1943, creating an avenue for women who are not members of Greek-letter organizations to serve alongside Sigma Gamma Rho alumnae chapters.

06

Rhoer Club

The Rhoer Club provides leadership development, educational enrichment, service, and mentoring for young women and remains one of Sigma Gamma Rho's internationally recognized affiliate organizations.

Our Purpose

Mission

It is the mission of Sigma Gamma Rho Sorority, Inc. to enhance the quality of life for women and their families in the U.S. and globally through community service, civil, and social action. Our goal is to achieve greater progress in the areas of education, health awareness, and leadership development. Our members, affiliates, staff, and community partners work to create and support initiatives that align with our vision.

What We Envision

Vision

We visualize a world in which all women and their families reach their full potential in all aspects of life and are able to create unlimited opportunities for future generations.

Our Journey

More Than a Century in Motion

From seven educators in Indianapolis to a national institution shaped by education, civic action, service, and organizational growth.

1922

Founding

Seven educators build an institution.

Sigma Gamma Rho Sorority, Inc. was founded on November 12, 1922, in Indianapolis, Indiana, by seven African American educators taking courses at Butler University: Mary Lou Allison Gardner Little, Dorothy Hanley Whiteside, Vivian Irene White Marbury, Nannie Mae Gahn Johnson, Hattie Mae Annette Dulin Redford, Bessie Mae Downey Rhoades Martin, and Cubena McClure.

At its beginning, the Sorority was organized as a professional sorority for Black female educators. Its founders emphasized continuing education, professional advancement, academic success for students, and expanding educational opportunity for Black women.

The Sorority developed during a period of severe racial discrimination in Indiana. Black students faced restricted access to higher education, while the Ku Klux Klan held significant political and social influence in the state. Against that backdrop, the creation of Sigma Gamma Rho was not incidental. It was an act of institution-building.

Legal Foundation

Robert Lee Brokenburr

Indianapolis attorney Robert Lee Brokenburr became legal advisor to the Sorority and filed incorporation papers in December 1922. Early membership was limited to teachers working in recognized schools, with members encouraged to continue their education and encourage others to do the same.

1925

Structure & Identity

The first Boulé establishes a national rhythm.

Over the next several years, the Sorority concentrated on building its organizational structure, defining its symbols, and establishing the practices that would carry Sigma Gamma Rho beyond its founding chapter.

The first national convention, the Boulé, was held in Indianapolis in 1925. At that gathering, the motto “Greater Progress, Greater Service” was introduced. It was later reordered into the phrase that has endured for generations: Greater Service, Greater Progress.

Greater Service, Greater Progress.
1929

National Expansion

A broader mission takes shape.

By the fifth Boulé in Cleveland, Ohio, members voted to amend the Articles of Incorporation so that the Sorority could expand beyond its original professional-educator framework.

A Charter Committee was formed to secure a charter for Alpha Chapter at Butler University, positioning Sigma Gamma Rho for continued growth as a national collegiate sorority.

Institutional Shift

From professional roots to national collegiate reach

The late 1920s marked a decisive transition: the organization preserved its educational foundation while creating a structure capable of expanding its membership and national presence.

1928–37

National Pan-Hellenic Council

Part of the early effort to coordinate Black Greek-letter organizations.

In December 1928, representatives of Alpha Phi Alpha, Kappa Alpha Psi, Omega Psi Phi, Alpha Kappa Alpha, Delta Sigma Theta, and Sigma Gamma Rho met in Indianapolis and agreed to create a temporary coordinating body as work continued toward a permanent national organization.

Historian André McKenzie later described that Indianapolis meeting as the “spiritual birth” of what became the National Pan-Hellenic Council.

The NPHC formally came into existence in 1930. Sigma Gamma Rho later rejoined the Council as a full member in 1937 after earlier participation in the organizing effort.

1937 Full NPHC membership
1930s

The Great Depression

Service responds to the realities of the moment.

During the Great Depression, Sigma Gamma Rho expanded educational and social services for African American families. Chapters sponsored literary contests that awarded books to Black children, developed vocational guidance programs, and created employment assistance efforts to help members locate work.

The Sorority also supported broader civil-rights efforts, including campaigns for federal anti-lynching legislation. Local chapters established employment bureaus, literacy classes, and health programs shaped by the needs of their communities.

01 Educational Access

Books, literacy work, and academic support.

02 Employment Support

Vocational guidance and employment-aid efforts.

03 Civil Action

Support for anti-lynching advocacy and racial justice.

1940s

World War II & Youth Programs

Service expands from wartime support to youth development.

During World War II, Sigma Gamma Rho worked alongside national organizations such as the American Red Cross and the U.S.O. and supported the Fair Employment Practices Committee, which challenged racial discrimination in defense employment.

The Sorority also developed youth-centered programming during this period. Sigma Teen Town provided supervised spaces where adolescents could gather, participate in activities, and build community. Local chapters adapted the model through teen canteens and other youth initiatives.

A Pattern Emerges

National purpose, local adaptation

A defining feature of Sigma Gamma Rho’s service tradition was already visible: national priorities were translated into programs flexible enough to meet local community needs.

Today

The Work Continues

A century later, the responsibility remains.

Sigma Gamma Rho’s history is not a sequence of dates detached from the present. It is a record of how the institution learned to organize, advocate, educate, serve, and expand without abandoning the principles that shaped its founding.

Each generation inherits that work.

More than a century later, the story is still in motion.

National Structure

A National Sisterhood. Five Regions.

The regional system connected a growing Sorority across chapters, campuses, communities, and generations of leadership.

Established 1925 • Organized 1940

Central

01

The first region to form and the region in which Sigma Gamma Rho itself was born.

Sorors in Central Region originated many of the Sorority's defining elements, including the Pledge, National Hymn, Slogan, Badge, Coat of Arms, Flower, and Colors.

December 29, 1939

Northeastern

02

Organized at the 14th Boulé when the Sorority expanded from two regions to four.

Soror Edna F. Browne, remembered as “The Mother of the Northeastern Region,” became its first Syntaktes.

Established 1939

Southeastern

03

Created during the Sorority's 1939 regional restructuring.

Soror Elizabeth Lemon served as the first Syntaktes as Sigma Gamma Rho strengthened its leadership structure throughout the Southeast.

Voted 1939 • Organized 1940

Southwestern

04

Organized under first Syntaktes Soror Mary Scales Greenwood of Houston.

Southwestern Region later contributed enduring traditions including the Poodle as official mascot and the development of the Philo Affiliate.

December 1952

Western

05

The fifth and most recently established of today's regions.

Formerly part of Southwestern Region, Western was organized under first Syntaktes Soror Lessie B. Crossland and held its first conference in 1953.

A Living Legacy

History That Still Requires Something of Us

Sigma Gamma Rho's history is not preserved merely to be admired. It establishes a standard.

Each generation inherits the responsibility to strengthen the institution, serve communities with purpose, and leave the sisterhood more capable than it found it.

“Our story did not start with a moment. It started with a decision. And it continues with each of us.”

Greater Service, Greater Progress.
